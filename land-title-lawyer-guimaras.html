A title is not a tax declaration

Most transfers in Guimaras involve a Torrens title under Presidential Decree No. 1529, the Property Registration Decree. The Registry of Deeds issues the title. The owner's duplicate is the copy the owner keeps. The municipal assessor issues a tax declaration so the local government can collect real property tax. A tax declaration shows who has been declaring the land and paying the tax. It is not, standing alone, a Torrens title. A buyer who receives only a tax declaration is not in the same position as a buyer who receives a title.

If the land has no title, the next step is not an ordinary deed of sale. It may be an application for a free patent or a petition for judicial confirmation of title. Possession, the classification of the land, and the papers already on file decide which application can be filed. The article on titling untitled inherited land explains that choice.

Buying or selling a titled lot

Before any payment, the papers to read are the original owner's duplicate of the title, the current tax declaration, the latest real property tax receipts, and every annotation on the title. An annotation may be a mortgage, a lien, an adverse claim, or a notice of lis pendens. A photocopy of a title is a starting point. It is not a substitute for the original duplicate and for a check with the Registry of Deeds of Guimaras.

A deed of absolute sale, a contract to sell, a donation, or a real estate mortgage has to be signed by the persons who have authority to sign, and it has to be notarized with those persons present. The notary public page explains personal appearance and identification. A notarized deed is still only one step. Capital gains tax or creditable withholding tax, documentary stamp tax, the electronic Certificate Authorizing Registration from the Bureau of Internal Revenue, transfer tax with the provincial treasurer, and registration with the Registry of Deeds are separate. The Registry issues the new title. The notary does not, and the office does not set the BIR or Registry schedule.

The article on buying land in Guimaras lists those checks in more detail.

When the registered owner has died

If the title is still in the name of a person who has died, the heirs cannot sign a deed of sale as if they were already the registered owners. Where there is no will, the estate has no outstanding debts, and the heirs agree, an extrajudicial settlement under Rule 74 of the Rules of Court is the usual deed. Minor heirs may take part if they are represented by their judicial or legal representatives. If there is a will, a disagreement, or a debt that has not been provided for, a court proceeding may be required instead. Estate tax and the BIR certificate come before the Registry will cancel the old title and issue a new one. The guide to extrajudicial settlement in Guimaras follows that sequence from the deed to the new title.

Annotations, a lost duplicate, and a path

An adverse claim is an annotation under Section 70 of PD 1529. It warns third persons that someone asserts an interest. It is not a judgment that the claimant owns the land, and it does not by itself cancel the registered owner's title. Cancelling it has its own requirements. Two articles discuss that annotation and should be read together: what an adverse claim is and how to cancel it, and an adverse claim on a Torrens title.

A lost owner's duplicate is not the same as a title that was never issued. Reissuance replaces the owner's copy when the Registry copy still exists. Reconstitution is used when the Registry's own copy is lost. The difference is in the article on reissuance versus reconstitution.

A path neighbors have used for years is a right-of-way question under the Civil Code. The conditions are in the article on whether an owner can close a path.

What to bring

Bring the title or the tax declaration, any deed already signed, tax receipts, and a short note on what you want to happen next: a sale, a transfer to heirs, removal of an annotation, or a question about a boundary. If a relative will sign for someone else, bring the authority that allows that relative to sign. The office will say which government office has the next step and which paper is missing. It will not say, before seeing the documents, that a title will be issued or that an annotation will be removed.
