Annulment and Nullity of Marriage in Guimaras

Edang Law Office, 2nd Floor, JACQ Commercial Building, San Miguel, Jordan, Guimaras.

People in Guimaras often use the word annulment for any case that ends a marriage in the civil registry. The Family Code uses two different petitions. A declaration of nullity is for a marriage that is void from the beginning. Annulment is for a voidable marriage, which is treated as valid until a court annuls it. Edang Law Office in Jordan handles both, together with questions of property, custody, and support that usually come with the petition. Clients come from Jordan, Buenavista, Nueva Valencia, San Lorenzo, and Sibunag, and from Iloilo. Meetings are in person at the 2nd Floor, JACQ Commercial Building, San Miguel, or online.

Void marriages

Articles 35, 36, 37, and 38 of the Family Code list marriages that are void. Examples include a marriage solemnized without a license except in the cases the Code itself exempts, a bigamous marriage, a marriage between persons within the prohibited degrees of relationship, and a marriage void for psychological incapacity under Article 36. A void marriage does not become valid by the passage of time. A court declaration is still what the civil registrar annotates on the record, and what either party relies on before a later marriage.

Voidable marriages

Article 45 lists the grounds for annulment of a voidable marriage. They include lack of parental consent where a party was eighteen or older but below twenty-one, unsoundness of mind, consent obtained by fraud of the kind Article 46 describes, consent obtained by force, intimidation, or undue influence, physical incapacity to consummate the marriage, and a serious and incurable sexually transmissible disease. These grounds are not open forever. The Code gives a period for filing, and the period is counted differently for each ground. A petition filed after that period can be dismissed even when the ground once existed.

A decree from a church is not an annulment under the Family Code. It has no effect, by itself, on civil status.

Psychological incapacity

Article 36 is a ground for a declaration of nullity, not for annulment under Article 45. In Tan-Andal v. Andal, G.R. No. 196359, May 11, 2021, the Supreme Court treated psychological incapacity as a legal concept: a grave and permanent inability to assume the essential duties of marriage, proved from the person's behavior, rather than a mental illness that a doctor must diagnose. Testimony from a psychologist can still be offered. It is not required in every case. Later decisions have applied that approach, including questions about who may file. The article on 2025 developments in nullity cases collects those points. This page does not repeat that case list.

Ordinary marital difficulty, or infidelity standing alone, is not automatically psychological incapacity. The court looks at whether the person was unable to meet the obligations of marriage, and at whether that inability existed at the time of the wedding even if it became obvious only later. A plain account of annulment and nullity, written for readers who are starting from the Family Code, is in Understanding Annulment in the Philippines.

A criminal complaint under Republic Act No. 9262 is a separate case. It does not annul the marriage. The articles on marital infidelity charged as psychological violence and on economic abuse describe that statute.

How a petition moves

The petition is filed in the Family Court that has venue. Venue depends on where the petitioner or the respondent has resided for the period the rule requires. This page does not name a court branch for Guimaras. The proper court is determined from those facts and from how cases are assigned. The petition states the ground and the facts. The public prosecutor takes part so the State can check for collusion and for evidence that was invented. There is a pre-trial, then presentation of witnesses, then a decision. If the decision becomes final, the civil registrar annotates the marriage record as the decision directs.

Property relations between the spouses, custody of children, and support are taken up in the same case or in orders issued in it. None of them follows automatically from the filing of a petition. The court decides them from the evidence and from the Family Code rules on the property regime that applied.

What a consultation can and cannot say

There is no standard number of months. Hearings depend on the court calendar, on whether the other spouse appears, and on the evidence. A first meeting cannot say that the petition will be granted. The professional fee depends on the ground and on the work the case will take, and it is stated after the facts are heard. It is not listed on this page. Legal separation, and a petition to recognize a divorce obtained by a foreign spouse, are different remedies. They are discussed when the facts fit them better than nullity or annulment.

Bring the marriage certificate, the birth certificates of the children, any written agreement about property or support, and a chronology of the marriage.
