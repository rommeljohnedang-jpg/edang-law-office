Notary Public in Jordan, Guimaras

Edang Law Office, 2nd Floor, JACQ Commercial Building, San Miguel, Jordan, Guimaras.

Atty. Rommel John G. Edang is a commissioned notary public. Notarization is done at Edang Law Office, 2nd Floor, JACQ Commercial Building, San Miguel, Jordan, Guimaras, Monday to Saturday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM.

People come from Jordan, Buenavista, Nueva Valencia, San Lorenzo, and Sibunag, and from Iloilo, to notarize papers for a Guimaras transaction. The signatory has to appear in person. A document cannot be left at the office for someone else to have notarized in the signatory's absence.

What is usually notarized

A notarial act is not the same thing as a lawyer drafting the document. Notarization is the act of a notary public, under the 2004 Rules on Notarial Practice, confirming that a person appeared, was identified, and signed or swore to a document. The office both prepares documents and notarizes documents that are already prepared, after reviewing them.

Documents commonly presented here include:

  • Affidavits, including affidavits of loss, support, and discrepancy in name
  • Acknowledgments on deeds of absolute sale, donations, and real estate mortgages
  • Special powers of attorney, including powers used for a land transfer or a bank transaction
  • Extrajudicial settlements and affidavits of self-adjudication, when the heirs are ready to sign
  • Contracts, lease agreements, and sworn statements for a government office or a court

If the paper is incomplete, unsigned by a person who is not present, or asks the notary to certify something the notary did not see, it will not be notarized. A notary does not notarize a blank page, a document whose blanks are still empty, or a document the signatory does not understand.

Personal appearance and identification

Under the 2004 Rules on Notarial Practice, the person who signs must be present before the notary at the time of the notarial act. The notary identifies that person through competent evidence of identity. In ordinary practice that is at least one current identification document issued by an official agency, bearing the person's photograph and signature. A Philippine passport, a driver's license, a UMID, a PhilSys ID, or another current government ID of that kind is the usual proof.

Bring the original ID, not a photograph of it on a phone, unless the notary can accept it for a particular act. The name on the ID should match the name on the document. If the names differ because of a marriage, a spelling difference, or a missing middle name, bring the paper that explains the difference, such as a marriage certificate or a birth certificate.

An instrument signed abroad is a different step. An SPA or deed signed outside the Philippines is generally apostilled or consularized in the country where it is signed. It is not notarized again in Jordan as a substitute for that foreign formality. The office can look at the foreign document and say what still has to be done in Guimaras.

What to bring

  • The document, or the facts needed if the office will prepare it
  • A current government ID for every person who will sign
  • Supporting papers the document mentions, such as a title, tax declaration, or birth certificate
  • The full name, address, and civil status of each signatory, as they should appear in the document
  • For a representative, the special power of attorney and the representative's own ID

Every person who must sign should come at the same time if the document requires all of their signatures. If one heir or one seller cannot come, that person needs a separate authority for someone else to sign, and that authority has its own formalities. Do not sign for another adult who simply could not make the trip, unless a valid authority is already in hand.

Fees, copies, and the notarial register

Notarial fees follow the schedule in the 2004 Rules on Notarial Practice. The amount depends on the kind of act and, for some documents, on the value stated in the instrument. The fee is stated before the act. The office does not add a separate charge for calling the document urgent, and it does not notarize outside the rules in exchange for a higher fee.

The notary enters the act in the notarial register. You should leave with a copy that shows the notarial details: the document number, page number, book number, and series year, together with the notary's signature and seal. Keep that copy. Government offices, banks, and the Registry of Deeds usually ask for it.

Where this fits with other work

A notarized deed is often only the first paper in a land transfer. Estate tax, the BIR electronic Certificate Authorizing Registration, transfer tax, and registration with the Registry of Deeds of Guimaras are separate steps. Those are described on the land titling and real estate page and in the article on extrajudicial settlement after a death.
