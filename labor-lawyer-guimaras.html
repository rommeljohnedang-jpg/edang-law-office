Just cause and authorized cause

The Labor Code does not leave the ending of employment to the employer's preference. A dismissal has to rest on a cause the Code names, and the employer has to follow the procedure that goes with that cause.

A just cause is a reason laid to the employee. Article 297, which earlier editions numbered as Article 282, lists serious misconduct, willful disobedience of a lawful order connected with the work, gross and habitual neglect of duties, fraud or willful breach of trust, a crime or offense against the employer or the employer's immediate family or representatives, and other causes analogous to those. For a just cause, due process means a written notice that specifies the acts, a genuine opportunity to explain and to be heard, and a written notice of the decision. A notice that does not state the acts, or a decision issued the same day as the first notice with no chance to answer, is a defect the employee can raise.

An authorized cause is a reason about the business rather than about misconduct. Article 298, formerly Article 283, covers installation of labor-saving devices, redundancy, retrenchment to prevent losses, and closing or cessation of operation. Article 299, formerly Article 284, covers disease where continued employment is prohibited by law or is prejudicial to health, under the conditions that article states. Authorized causes require written notice to the employee and to the Department of Labor and Employment at least thirty days before the effective date. Separation pay is due in the cases and in the amounts the Code provides. It is not due in every closure, and the amount for redundancy is not the same as the amount for retrenchment. An employer who uses the word redundancy, but does not show that the position is in excess of what the business needs, has not established the ground.

If a dismissal is declared illegal

Where a labor tribunal finds that the dismissal was illegal, the Labor Code provides reinstatement to the former position without loss of seniority rights, and full backwages, counted from the time compensation was withheld up to actual reinstatement. When reinstatement is no longer viable, separation pay may be awarded instead. Those are remedies ordered after a finding. They are not a result the office can state as certain at a first meeting. A settlement, if there is one, is an agreement between the parties rather than that award.

Where a complaint is filed

Illegal dismissal and many money claims are filed with the National Labor Relations Commission. In most cases the filing is preceded by the Single Entry Approach at the Department of Labor and Employment, a conciliation step that asks the parties to try to settle before a formal case. DOLE also looks into labor-standards questions, including unpaid wages, through its visitorial and enforcement authority. Which office is the right one depends on the relief being asked for.

Deadlines are short. Money claims arising from the Labor Code generally prescribe in three years under Article 306, formerly Article 291. An action for illegal dismissal has been treated by the Supreme Court as prescribing in four years. The count usually runs from the date of dismissal, or from the date a benefit should have been paid. Bring the papers in time for a consultation to check the date. A claim that has prescribed is dismissed even when the notices were defective.

Reassignment and forced leave

A transfer to another post, or a forced leave with no work and no pay, can be a management step or a way of pushing an employee out. The difference is whether the transfer was reasonable and in good faith, and whether rank, pay, or benefits were cut. The article on unfair reassignment and forced leave goes through those facts. The article on what to do if you are illegally dismissed lists the papers to keep: the termination notice, the contract, payslips, and the messages that show when the work ended. Do not sign a quitclaim without reading which claims it waives. A quitclaim is not always final, but signing one makes the later case harder.

What to bring

Bring the employment contract, company identification, payslips, the notices, any memo on absence or reassignment, and a list of dates. The office will say which forum fits and what the notices appear to omit. It will not promise reinstatement, a peso amount, or a hearing on a particular week. Questions people ask

The 30-day notice to the employee and to DOLE applies to authorized causes. A just-cause dismissal uses a different notice procedure. The ground still has to be proved.

Reinstatement and backwages are the remedies the law provides when a dismissal is found illegal. They are ordered after that finding. They are not promised at the first meeting.

Illegal dismissal cases generally go to the NLRC, usually after the Single Entry Approach at DOLE. The right office depends on the relief being asked for.

Money claims generally prescribe in three years, and illegal dismissal has been treated as prescribing in four years. Bring the papers before those periods run.
